Unit 1 - INTRODUCTION TO HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT

Concept, objectives, scope, functions, challenges, SHRM and HRM models

1. Meaning of Human Resource Management

Definition - Human Resource Management (HRM): HRM is the systematic process of acquiring, developing, motivating, compensating and retaining people so that individual and organizational goals are achieved.
Simple Meaning: HRM = Getting the right people + Developing them + Motivating them + Retaining them.
Basic HRM Flow Human Resource Planning ↓ Recruitment and Selection ↓ Training and Performance ↓ Compensation and Employee Relations ↓ Retention and Organizational Success
Simple Example: A company estimates that it needs 20 sales executives, recruits suitable candidates, trains them, fixes performance targets, pays incentives and retains the best performers. The complete process is HRM.

2. Nature and Features of HRM

  • People-oriented: HRM deals with employees as valuable organizational resources.
  • Pervasive: Every manager performs HR activities, not only the HR department.
  • Continuous: Recruitment, training, appraisal and motivation continue throughout organizational life.
  • Goal-oriented: HRM connects employee contribution with organizational objectives.
  • Development-oriented: It improves employee knowledge, skills, attitude and potential.
  • Integrative: It balances organizational goals with employee needs.
  • Strategic: Modern HRM supports long-term business strategy and competitive advantage.
  • Multidisciplinary: It uses knowledge from psychology, sociology, economics, law and management.
  • Future-oriented: It prepares employees and leadership for future changes.
  • Action-oriented: It focuses on practical policies and decisions rather than only record keeping.
Memory Trick: P-C-G-D-I-S-M-F-A = People, Continuous, Goal, Development, Integrative, Strategic, Multidisciplinary, Future, Action.

3. Objectives of HRM

Main Objective: Organizational effectiveness through capable and satisfied employees.

  • Organizational objective: Help the organization achieve productivity, quality and growth.
  • Functional objective: Maintain the HR department at an appropriate and useful level.
  • Personal objective: Support employee career, income, recognition and development.
  • Societal objective: Follow laws, ethics, equality and social responsibility.
  • Place the right person in the right job.
  • Develop employee competence through training.
  • Build motivation, commitment and job satisfaction.
  • Maintain sound employee and industrial relations.
  • Control labour cost and improve productivity.
  • Retain talented employees and reduce avoidable turnover.
Four-Level Objective Model Societal Objectives ↓ Organizational Objectives ↓ Functional Objectives ↓ Personal Objectives

  • Improves productivity and quality of work.
  • Reduces labour turnover, absenteeism and conflict.
  • Builds employee morale, motivation and commitment.
  • Supports innovation, change and technology adoption.
  • Creates a positive work culture.
  • Helps legal compliance and ethical employment practices.
  • Develops future managers and leaders.
  • Improves employer image and talent attraction.
  • Creates sustainable competitive advantage.

6. Functions of HRM

A. Managerial Functions

  • Planning: Estimating workforce needs and deciding HR policies.
  • Organizing: Designing HR structure, roles and authority.
  • Directing: Guiding, motivating, communicating and leading employees.
  • Controlling: Comparing HR results with standards and taking corrective action.

B. Operative Functions

  • Procurement of employees.
  • Training and development.
  • Performance appraisal and performance management.
  • Compensation and benefits.
  • Employee welfare, health and safety.
  • Industrial and employee relations.
  • HR records, HRIS and compliance.
  • Separation and exit management.

C. Advisory Functions

  • Advising top management on workforce strategy.
  • Advising line managers on recruitment, discipline, performance and employee issues.
Functions of HRM Managerial Functions: Planning - Organizing - Directing - Controlling + Operative Functions: Procurement - Development - Compensation - Integration - Maintenance - Separation
Memory Trick: PODC + PDCIMS = Planning, Organizing, Directing, Controlling + Procurement, Development, Compensation, Integration, Maintenance, Separation.

  • Rapid technological change, automation and artificial intelligence.
  • Shortage of critical skills and intense competition for talent.

12. Strategic Human Resource Management (SHRM)

Definition - SHRM: Strategic Human Resource Management is the planned alignment of HR policies and practices with the long-term strategy of the organization.
Simple Meaning: SHRM = Business strategy and people strategy moving in the same direction.

Nature and Importance of SHRM

  • Long-term and future-oriented.
  • Integrates HR decisions with business decisions.
  • Focuses on organizational performance and competitive advantage.
  • Ensures the right skills are available for future strategy.
  • Builds culture, leadership and change capability.
  • Uses workforce measures and evidence for decisions.
  • Creates consistency among recruitment, appraisal, rewards and development.
Strategic Alignment Business Vision and Strategy ↓ Required Organization Capabilities ↓ HR Strategy ↓ Recruitment - Development - Performance - Rewards ↓ Business Results

13. HRM Models

A. Harvard Model

The Harvard Model is a stakeholder-oriented model. It states that HR decisions are affected by stakeholder interests and situational factors. HR policy choices then produce employee and long-term organizational outcomes.

Harvard Model Stakeholder Interests + Situational Factors ↓ HR Policy Choices (Employee Influence, HR Flow, Rewards, Work Systems) ↓ HR Outcomes (Commitment, Competence, Congruence, Cost-effectiveness) ↓ Long-Term Individual, Organizational and Social Results
4C Outcomes: Commitment - Competence - Congruence - Cost-effectiveness.

B. Matching Model (Michigan Model)

The Matching Model emphasizes a strong fit between business strategy, organization structure and HR systems.

Matching Model Business Strategy and Organization Structure ↓ Selection → Performance Appraisal → Rewards → Development ↓ Improved Individual and Organizational Performance

Harvard Model vs Matching Model

BasisHarvard ModelMatching Model
ApproachSoft, stakeholder and employee-orientedHard, strategic fit-oriented
FocusBalancing multiple interestsMatching HR systems with business strategy
Main outcomesCommitment, competence, congruence, cost-effectivenessPerformance through selection, appraisal, rewards and development
ViewBroad and pluralisticManagerial and performance-centred

Q1. Define Human Resource Management and explain its objectives and functions.

Introduction: Human Resource Management is a systematic approach to managing people so that organizational and individual goals are achieved.

  1. Objectives: Achieve organizational goals; maintain the HR function; support employee growth; meet social and legal responsibilities.
  2. Planning: Estimate future workforce requirements and frame suitable HR policies.
  3. Organizing: Design roles, authority and HR department relationships.
  4. Directing: Motivate, communicate, lead and guide employees.
  5. Controlling: Measure HR results and take corrective action.
  6. Procurement: Recruit, select, place and induct the right employees.
  7. Development: Train employees and develop future leadership.
  8. Compensation: Provide fair wages, incentives and benefits.
  9. Integration and maintenance: Build motivation, welfare, safety and healthy relations.
  10. Separation: Manage retirement, resignation and other exits.
Example: For example, an IT company uses workforce planning, campus recruitment, training, performance-linked rewards and career paths to build and retain software talent.

Conclusion: Thus, HRM converts employee capability into organizational performance while also protecting employee interests.

Q2. Explain Strategic Human Resource Management and the Harvard and Matching Models.

Introduction: SHRM links people-management decisions with long-term business strategy. It ensures that workforce capability supports the future direction of the organization.

  1. Strategic alignment: HR priorities are derived from business goals.
  2. Long-term focus: SHRM anticipates future skills, leadership and culture needs.
  3. Integration: Recruitment, training, appraisal and rewards support the same strategic direction.
  4. Competitive advantage: Unique employee capability is difficult for competitors to copy.
  5. Harvard Model: Considers stakeholders and situational factors; HR policy choices produce the 4C outcomes and long-term consequences.
  6. Matching Model: Matches selection, appraisal, rewards and development with strategy and structure.
  7. Comparison: Harvard is broader and stakeholder-oriented; Matching is more performance and fit-oriented.

Conclusion: Both models show that HRM is not only an administrative activity; it is a central part of organizational strategy.

Unit 2 - HUMAN RESOURCE PLANNING, ACQUISITION AND RETENTION

HRP, job analysis/design, recruitment, selection, induction, career and employee retention

1. Human Resource Planning (HRP)

Definition - Human Resource Planning: HRP is the process of forecasting an organization's future demand and supply of employees and preparing action plans to remove shortages or surpluses.
Simple Meaning: HRP = How many people? What skills? At what place? At what time?
HRP Balance Future HR Demand ↘ Compare → Gap ↗ Future HR Supply ↓ Recruit / Train / Transfer / Redeploy / Reduce

2. Objectives, Need and Importance of HRP

  • Ensure the right number and type of employees.
  • Avoid manpower shortage and surplus.
  • Support expansion, diversification and technology plans.
  • Estimate recruitment and training requirements.
  • Prepare succession and replacement plans.
  • Control labour cost.
  • Improve utilization of existing employees.
  • Manage retirements, transfers and employee turnover.
  • Support diversity and critical-skill planning.
  • Provide a base for HR budgets and policies.

3. Human Resource Planning Process

  1. Study organizational objectives and business plans.
  2. Analyze the current workforce through HR inventory and skill inventory.
  3. Forecast future human resource demand.
  4. Forecast internal and external human resource supply.
  5. Compare demand with supply and identify gaps.
  6. Prepare action plans: recruitment, training, redeployment, succession or downsizing.
  7. Implement the plan.
  8. Monitor, review and revise the HR plan.
Memory Trick: O-I-D-S-G-A-I-R = Objectives, Inventory, Demand, Supply, Gap, Action, Implement, Review.

5. Barriers to Effective HRP

  • Uncertain business environment.
  • Inaccurate or incomplete employee data.
  • Weak support from top management.
  • Lack of coordination between HR and line managers.
  • Rapid change in skills and technology.
  • Resistance to workforce changes.
  • Overemphasis on numbers and neglect of quality.
  • Difficulty in forecasting employee behaviour and turnover.

6. Job Analysis

Definition - Job Analysis: Job analysis is the systematic collection and study of information about a job's tasks, duties, responsibilities, working conditions and human requirements.
Simple Meaning: Job analysis studies the job, not the person.
Outputs of Job Analysis Job Analysis ├── Job Description: What the job contains └── Job Specification: What the job holder must possess

7. Job Analysis Process

  1. Decide the purpose of job analysis.
  2. Select the jobs to be analyzed.
  3. Collect background information and organization charts.
  4. Choose data collection methods.
  5. Collect job data from employees, supervisors and records.
  6. Verify the information.
  7. Prepare job description and job specification.
  8. Update the information when work changes.

15. Induction and Orientation

Definition - Induction: Induction is the formal process of introducing a new employee to the organization, its culture, policies, people and job.
Simple Meaning: Induction helps the employee enter the organization smoothly.

19. Employee Retention

Definition - Employee Retention: Employee retention refers to the organization's ability and efforts to keep valuable employees for a desirable period.
Simple Meaning: Retention means keeping capable employees engaged and willing to stay.

Why Employees Leave

  • Unfair pay or limited growth.
  • Poor supervisor relationship.
  • Work overload and stress.
  • Lack of recognition.
  • Weak job fit or unclear role.
  • Unhealthy culture or unfair treatment.
  • Better external opportunities.
  • Inflexible work and poor work-life balance.

Retention Strategies

  • Fair and competitive compensation.
  • Career growth, learning and internal mobility.
  • Supportive leadership and manager capability.
  • Recognition and meaningful rewards.
  • Employee engagement and voice.
  • Flexible work and well-being support.
  • Fair performance management.
  • Strong onboarding and realistic job preview.
  • Positive culture, inclusion and respect.
  • Stay interviews and timely correction of problems.
Retention Chain Good Job Fit + Fair Rewards + Growth + Supportive Manager ↓ Engagement and Commitment ↓ Higher Retention and Performance

Q1. Explain Human Resource Planning and its process.

Introduction: Human Resource Planning ensures that the organization has the right people with the right skills at the right time and cost.

  1. Study objectives: Understand strategy, expansion, technology and productivity plans.
  2. Current HR inventory: Analyze number, skills, age, experience, performance and potential of employees.
  3. Demand forecast: Estimate future quantity and quality of employees needed.
  4. Supply forecast: Estimate internal availability and external labour-market supply.
  5. Gap analysis: Identify shortages, surpluses and skill gaps.
  6. Action plans: Use recruitment, training, transfer, succession, outsourcing or workforce reduction.
  7. Implementation: Assign responsibility, budget and time schedules.
  8. Review: Compare results with plans and update forecasts.

Conclusion: Effective HRP reduces labour cost and ensures smooth execution of business strategy.

Q2. Explain Job Analysis, Job Description and Job Specification.

Introduction: Job analysis systematically studies the content and requirements of a job. Its two major outputs are job description and job specification.

  1. Purpose: Establish role clarity and provide reliable HR information.
  2. Data collection: Use observation, interview, questionnaire, diary and expert methods.
  3. Job description: Records title, purpose, duties, responsibilities, reporting, conditions and standards.
  4. Job specification: Records education, experience, knowledge, skills, abilities and personal requirements.
  5. Difference: Description explains the job; specification explains the required person.
  6. Uses: HR planning, recruitment, selection, training, appraisal, compensation and safety.
  7. Updating: Documents must be revised when technology or workflow changes.

Conclusion: Correct job information is the foundation of nearly every major HR decision.

Q3. Explain recruitment and selection processes and distinguish between them.

Introduction: Recruitment attracts qualified applicants, while selection evaluates applicants and appoints the most suitable candidate.

  1. Vacancy and job requirements: Confirm need and use job analysis information.
  2. Recruitment sources: Choose internal and/or external sources.
  3. Communication: Advertise the vacancy clearly and attract suitable applicants.
  4. Screening: Remove clearly unsuitable applications.
  5. Testing and interviews: Measure job-related ability, behaviour and fit.
  6. Verification: Check references, background and medical fitness where relevant.
  7. Final decision and offer: Select the best fit and issue appointment terms.
  8. Induction: Introduce the new employee to the job and organization.
  9. Difference: Recruitment is positive and pool-building; selection is evaluative and choice-making.

Conclusion: A valid, fair and structured recruitment-selection system improves performance and reduces early turnover.

Unit 3 - MANAGING EMPLOYEE PERFORMANCE AND TRAINING

Performance appraisal, performance management, potential appraisal, training, development and evaluation

1. Performance Appraisal

Definition - Performance Appraisal: Performance appraisal is the periodic and systematic evaluation of an employee's past performance against predetermined standards.
Simple Meaning: Performance appraisal judges how well an employee performed during a period.

2. Performance Management

Definition - Performance Management: Performance management is a continuous process of planning performance, coaching, reviewing results and developing employees to achieve organizational goals.
Simple Meaning: Performance management = Plan + Perform + Review + Improve.
Performance Management Cycle Set Goals and Standards ↓ Continuous Coaching and Support ↓ Measure and Review Performance ↓ Feedback, Rewards and Development ↺

3. Objectives and Importance

  • Clarify employee goals and expected standards.
  • Measure individual contribution.
  • Provide constructive feedback.
  • Identify strengths and development needs.
  • Support promotion, transfer and succession decisions.
  • Provide a basis for rewards and incentives.
  • Improve communication between manager and employee.
  • Correct poor performance.
  • Align individual goals with organizational strategy.
  • Document performance decisions fairly.

4. Performance Appraisal Process

  1. Define job responsibilities and performance standards.
  2. Communicate standards to employees.
  3. Select suitable appraisal methods and period.
  4. Observe and measure actual performance.
  5. Compare actual performance with standards.
  6. Conduct the appraisal discussion.
  7. Decide rewards, improvement or development action.
  8. Follow up and review progress.
Memory Trick: S-C-M-M-C-D-A-F = Standards, Communicate, Method, Measure, Compare, Discuss, Action, Follow-up.

Q1. Explain performance appraisal process and major appraisal methods.

Introduction: Performance appraisal systematically evaluates employee performance against job-related standards and supports feedback, rewards and development decisions.

  1. Set standards: Define expected results, competencies and behaviours.
  2. Communicate: Ensure employees understand goals and measures.
  3. Measure: Collect evidence through observation, records and results.
  4. Compare: Compare actual performance with standards.
  5. Discuss: Conduct a two-way appraisal interview.
  6. Action: Decide recognition, development or performance improvement.
  7. Traditional methods: Ranking, rating scales, checklist, critical incident and confidential report.
  8. Modern methods: MBO, BARS, 360-degree feedback and assessment centre.
  9. Control errors: Train raters and use multiple sources and clear standards.

Conclusion: A fair appraisal system should improve performance, not merely produce a score.

Q2. Explain training and development process and training methods.

Introduction: Training improves present-job performance, while development builds long-term capability. A systematic training process begins with need assessment and ends with evaluation and transfer of learning.

  1. Need assessment: Analyze organization, job and person gaps.
  2. Objectives: State measurable learning outcomes.
  3. Design: Select content, trainer, method, schedule and resources.
  4. Delivery: Conduct learning with practice and participation.
  5. On-the-job methods: Coaching, mentoring, rotation, apprenticeship and understudy.
  6. Off-the-job methods: Lecture, case, role play, simulation, vestibule and e-learning.
  7. Evaluation: Measure reaction, learning, behaviour and results.
  8. Follow-up: Provide supervisor support so learning is used on the job.

Conclusion: Training becomes valuable only when acquired learning improves workplace behaviour and organizational results.

Q3. Explain the Kirkpatrick Model of training evaluation.

Introduction: The Kirkpatrick Model evaluates training at four progressively stronger levels.

  1. Reaction: Measures learner satisfaction, engagement and perceived relevance.
  2. Learning: Measures knowledge, skills, attitude or confidence gained.
  3. Behaviour: Checks whether learning is applied in actual work.
  4. Results: Measures impact on productivity, quality, sales, cost, safety or other business outcomes.
  5. Use: Organizations should define desired results before training and collect evidence at each level.
  6. Limitation: Higher-level results may be affected by factors other than training, so careful measurement is required.

Conclusion: The model helps HR move from simply counting participants to proving whether training created useful organizational change.

1. Meaning of Compensation

Definition - Compensation: Compensation is the total financial and non-financial return provided to an employee in exchange for work and contribution.
Simple Meaning: Compensation = Pay + Incentives + Benefits + Recognition.

Q1. Explain HRIS, its need, uses, advantages and limitations.

Introduction: HRIS integrates HR data, processes and technology to improve HR administration and decision making.

  1. Need: Central records, faster transactions, better control and reliable workforce information.
  2. Core uses: Employee records, payroll, attendance, recruitment, learning, performance and benefits.
  3. Decision support: Reports and dashboards support planning and analysis.
  4. Employee self-service: Employees update data, apply leave and access documents.
  5. Advantages: Speed, accuracy, consistency, reduced paperwork and improved reporting.
  6. Limitations: Cost, poor data, privacy risk, resistance, vendor dependence and need for training.
  7. Implementation: Requirements, process mapping, system selection, data migration, testing, training and review.

Conclusion: HRIS creates value only when accurate data, sound processes, security and user adoption are managed together.

Q2. Explain Human Resource Accounting and its methods.

Introduction: Human Resource Accounting attempts to identify, measure and report investment in and value of human resources.

  1. Objectives: Support HR investment decisions, planning and management attention to human capability.
  2. Historical cost: Uses actual recruitment, training and development cost.
  3. Replacement cost: Uses estimated cost of replacing existing employees.
  4. Opportunity cost: Uses value of the best alternative internal use of scarce talent.
  5. Present value method: Discounts expected future earnings or benefits.
  6. Advantages: Highlights people as value-creating resources and supports long-term decisions.
  7. Limitations: Human value is uncertain, methods differ and assumptions can be subjective.

Conclusion: HRA is best used as supplementary management information rather than as an exact measure of human worth.

Q3. Explain HR Audit and its process.

Introduction: HR audit systematically evaluates HR policies, practices, records and results for compliance and effectiveness.

  1. Scope and standards: Define what will be audited and the criteria.
  2. Evidence collection: Review records, reports, policies, interviews and employee feedback.
  3. Evaluation: Compare practice with legal, policy and benchmark requirements.
  4. Risk identification: Classify gaps according to seriousness and impact.
  5. Reporting: Present strengths, findings and practical recommendations.
  6. Action plan: Assign responsibility and deadlines.
  7. Follow-up: Confirm correction and continuous improvement.
  8. Benefits: Better compliance, efficiency, accountability and strategic alignment.

Conclusion: An HR audit is not merely fault finding; it is a structured tool for improving the complete HR system.

Q4. Explain HR Shared Services, its benefits and implementation issues.

Introduction: HR shared services centralize routine HR processes for multiple business units while specialists and HR business partners focus on expertise and strategy.

  1. Coverage: Employee queries, records, payroll support, leave, letters and standard transactions.
  2. Benefits: Lower duplication, standardization, consistent service, better technology and reporting.
  3. Service model: Shared services work with centres of expertise, HR business partners and digital self-service.
  4. Issues: Resistance, loss of local touch, unclear roles, poor technology and service-quality concerns.
  5. Success conditions: Standard processes, service-level agreements, governance, capable staff and change management.

Conclusion: Shared services succeed when efficiency is balanced with responsive and human-centred employee service.
